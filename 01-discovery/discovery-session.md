# Discovery Session

## Purpose
Explore the problem space, understand stakeholder needs, and determine whether this project is worth pursuing. Discovery produces the raw material that Product Definition will refine into formal requirements.

## Agent Persona
[Discovery Agent](discovery-agent.md)

## Instructions

### Step 1: Problem Framing
Guide the stakeholder through articulating the problem:
- **What problem are we solving?** — State it in one sentence
- **Who has this problem?** — Identify the affected users/stakeholders
- **What's the current workaround?** — How do people cope today?
- **What's the cost of the status quo?** — Quantify the pain (time, money, risk, frustration)
- **Why now?** — What has changed to make this project timely?

### Step 2: Vision & Goals
Establish what success looks like:
- **Vision statement** — One paragraph describing the desired future state
- **Primary goals** (max 3) — What must this project achieve to be considered successful?
- **Non-goals** — What is explicitly out of scope? (This is as important as the goals)
- **Success metrics** — How will we measure whether we achieved the goals?

### Step 3: Stakeholder Mapping
Identify everyone who has a stake in this project:
- **Decision makers** — Who approves scope, budget, timeline?
- **Users** — Who will interact with the system daily?
- **Subject-matter experts** — Who understands the domain deeply?
- **Affected parties** — Who is impacted but not directly using the system?
- **Constraints owners** — Who controls budget, compliance, infrastructure?

### Step 4: Competitive & Landscape Analysis
Understand what already exists:
- **Existing solutions** — What tools/products address this problem today?
- **Why they're insufficient** — What gaps do they leave?
- **Differentiators** — What would make our solution distinct?
- **Risks of building** — What happens if we build and it's not better?

### Step 5: Initial Volatility Signals
Even at discovery, begin thinking about change:
- **What aspects of this problem are likely to evolve?**
- **What external factors could change the requirements?** (regulation, market, technology)
- **What assumptions are we making that might prove wrong?**
- **Where is the domain knowledge most uncertain?**

These signals will feed the volatility analysis in the Architecture phase.

## Inputs
- Stakeholder access (interviews, workshops, documents)
- Domain context (industry knowledge, existing systems)
- Business context (strategy, budget, timeline expectations)

## Outputs
- **Discovery Brief** — Structured summary of Steps 1-5 findings
- **Stakeholder Map** — Who's involved and their roles
- **Initial Risk Register** — Known risks with preliminary assessment
- **Go/No-Go Recommendation** — Based on feasibility assessment
- **Volatility Signals** — Early indicators of likely change axes

## Quality Criteria
- Problem statement is specific and testable (not vague)
- Goals are measurable (not "make it better")
- At least one success metric is quantifiable
- Non-goals are explicitly stated
- Key assumptions are called out as assumptions, not facts
- Stakeholder map is complete (no obvious gaps)
