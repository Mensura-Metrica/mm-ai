# Gate 1: Discovery → Product Definition

## Gate Purpose
Verify that discovery has produced sufficient understanding to begin formal product definition. Proceeding without adequate discovery leads to building the wrong thing.

## Checklist

### Problem Understanding
- [ ] Problem statement is specific and falsifiable — *Can you explain the problem in one sentence without jargon?*
- [ ] Affected users/stakeholders are identified — *Is the stakeholder map complete?*
- [ ] Current state (workarounds, pain points) is documented — *Do we know what people do today?*
- [ ] Cost of status quo is estimated — *Can we quantify the pain?*

### Vision & Scope
- [ ] Vision statement exists and is clear — *Does the team agree on the desired future state?*
- [ ] Primary goals (max 3) are defined and measurable — *Can each goal be verified as achieved or not?*
- [ ] Non-goals are explicitly stated — *Do we know what we're NOT building?*
- [ ] At least one success metric is quantifiable — *How will we know we succeeded?*

### Feasibility
- [ ] Technical feasibility is assessed — *Are there any showstoppers?*
- [ ] Economic feasibility is assessed — *Does the ROI make sense?*
- [ ] Organizational feasibility is assessed — *Can this team execute?*
- [ ] Go/No-Go recommendation is documented — *Is there a clear recommendation?*

### Risk & Volatility
- [ ] Initial risk register exists — *Are the top risks identified?*
- [ ] Volatility signals are documented — *Do we know what's likely to change?*
- [ ] Key assumptions are called out explicitly — *What are we assuming but don't know for sure?*

## Gate Decision
- **PASS**: All items checked. Proceed to Product Definition.
- **FAIL**: Return to Discovery to address gaps. Document which items failed and why.

## Gate Owner
The human stakeholder makes the final gate decision. The LLM agent presents the evidence and recommendation.
